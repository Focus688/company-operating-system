# 10 · People & the Iceberg Model

> Thread: every layer starts with people.
> Matching people has two layers — above the waterline (visible capability) and below it (invisible values/motivation/personality).
> Hire the wrong person and the above-water part can be trained; the below-water part almost never changes.

---

## 1. The Iceberg Model

```
        ┌─────────────────────────┐
        │ Knowledge / Skills /    │ ← Above the waterline: visible, trainable, measurable
        │ Capability              │
        │ (degrees/certs/results) │
        ├─────────────────────────┤
        │ Values / Motivation /   │ ← Below the waterline: invisible, hard to change,
        │ Personality             │   determines fit
        │ (culture fit/drivers)   │
        └─────────────────────────┘
```

| Layer | Content | Can it change? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Above the waterline | Knowledge, skills, capability | ✅ Trainable (results visible in 3–6 months) |
| Below the waterline | Values, motivation, personality, cultural fit | ❌ Almost never (stable once adult) |

---

## 2. Hiring: Look at Both Sides of the Iceberg

| Stage | What to look at |
|:---|:---|
| Resume | Above the waterline (experience/skills) |
| Test / work sample | Above the waterline (capability validation) |
| Deep-dive interview | Below the waterline (motivation/values/personality) — behavioral interviewing |
| Reference check | Below the waterline (reputation/stability) |
| Probation period | Both sides, combined validation |

**Behavioral interviewing** (ask about the past, not hypotheticals):
- ❌ "What would you do if X happened?" (hypothetical — unreliable)
- ✅ "Last time X happened, what did you actually do?" (past behavior predicts future behavior)

---

## 3. Deploying People: The Right Person in the Right Seat

- **Play to strengths, tolerate weaknesses** (Drucker)
- Key roles require both sides to match (capability is enough + values align)
- Regular roles: capability clears the bar; values are a preference
- **Promotions hinge on performance + values** (great results but misaligned values = a ticking time bomb)

---

## 4. Retention: Money + Growth + Meaning

| Layer | Content | Who it's for |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Money | Pay, bonuses, equity | Everyone (the baseline) |
| Growth | Promotion, learning, challenge | Ambitious high-achievers |
| Meaning | Mission, recognition, belonging | Value-driven people |

- Core talent: equity lock-in (aligned interests)
- Backbone: promotion paths + learning opportunities
- Frontline: fair pay + respect

---

## 5. Making Culture Real (organizing what's below the waterline)

Values aren't words on a wall — they are **mechanisms**:

- **Hiring bar**: values don't fit → don't hire, no matter how strong the candidate
- **Performance review**: values are scored (performance + values, two dimensions)
- **Rewards & consequences**: publicly reward behavior that embodies the values
- **Rituals & stories**: turn values into stories people can tell
- **Leaders model the way**: culture is a mirror of the boss

---

## 6. Cases

| Company | Practice | Lesson |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Alibaba (Chinese e-commerce giant) | "Six Vein Sword" values-based reviews ("wild dogs" = strong results, poor values → removed) | Values are the floor |
| Huawei (Chinese telecom equipment maker) | People-first for strivers; equity + high pay to keep aligned people | Lock people in with mechanisms |
| Haidilao (Chinese hot pot chain) | Apprenticeship system + service culture | What's below the waterline becomes a competitive advantage |
| Google | Hiring committees + structured interviews | Systematize the hiring process |

---

> 📌 **One-line summary**: Hire for what's below the waterline, train for what's above — someone whose values don't fit is a liability no matter how capable.
